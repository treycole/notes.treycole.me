#### Synopsis

Annihilation follows a cellular biology professor and former U.S. army soldier, Lena, whose army sergeant husband, presumed dead, miraculously reappears after being gone for more than a year on a classified mission. During the morning after their reuniting, his health quickly degrades, prompting a frantic trip to the hospital. Along the journey to the hospital, the ambulance is stopped by agents who quickly whisk him away, sedating Lena, and taking her with them. She wakes at area X, a secret location on the outskirts of a prismatic field, called the Shimmer, surrounding a lighthouse that was revealed to have been struck by an extraterrestrial meteor. It is quickly engulfing the area, and nobody who has entered the field has returned alive, until her husband, who is now in critical condition. To save her husband, she volunteers to join a group of four consisting of a psychologist, a paramedic, a physicist, and a geomorphologist, entering the Shimmer with hopes of finding the source and cause of this spreading field. 

Upon entering the field, their memory is unstable, and they are unable to recall how long they have been there. Their compasses are no longer aligned with Earth's magnetic field, and none of their electromagnetic communications are getting through to the base. The land is scattered with strange plants and creatures with mutations that indicate their DNA is somehow being merged with the surroundings and each other, becoming homogenized. Animals appear to come in pairs, behaving identically with subtly different appearances. They soon find that they are themselves undergoing the same kinds of mutations, and evidenced by their cells splitting into two visually different forms, and their skin actively swirling and morphing. 

After observing the plants growing into a humanoid shape, the physicist comes to the conclusion that the Shimmer is a kind of prism that refracts everything within, including the DNA of the lifeforms in the Shimmer. She uses this hypothesis to explain why the plants have inherited features of human DNA, and how other lifeforms appear to share characteristics with each other. She also uses this hypothesis to explain why their electromagnetic communications are not exiting the Shimmer.

### The physics

#### Diffraction of DNA?

Now let's dissect this claim in the film, that their DNA is being refracted and why some of the animals may appear in pairs. Diffraction and refraction are names given to the phenomenon of light changing its speed, and consequently its direction, when passing from one material to the next. Using Maxwell's electromagnetic equations, and knowing the material properties, the angle between the incident and transmitted light can be computed from the boundary conditions at the interface between the materials. 

How could DNA be diffracted? Diffraction and refraction are properties of waves, not particles or solids like DNA molecules. For any diffraction or refraction to be possible here, we have to invoke quantum mechanics which tells us that particles often exhibit wave-like properties and can exhibit interference, diffraction, and refraction. The film never explicitly mentions this, and so this is left unexplained. 

#### Birefringence 

An interest aspect of the film is the pairs of animals. The science they use in the film is largely about electrodynamics and optics. While its not mentioned, this pairing does actually have an interesting electromagnetic analogy called birefringence. Birefringence is when a single beam of light is split into two after entering the material, their paths diverging from each other, only to be recombined after exiting the material. Clearly animal life forms behave quite differently from light, and so the idea that this could occur at the macroscopic scale with materials is highly implausible. However, if we accept that DNA can refract and diffract, we can also accept that animals can undergo birefringence. This explains why they observe some of the animals coming in pairs, moving and acting identically.
